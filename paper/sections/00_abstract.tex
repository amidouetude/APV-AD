\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Land-use competition between food and renewable energy is a pressing
challenge in semi-arid regions. This study develops a coupled six-model
optimization framework for bifacial agrivoltaic (APV) and on-farm anaerobic
digestion (AD) across four climate zones --- Konya (BSk), Almer\'{i}a
(BSh), Ouagadougou (BSh), and Freiburg (Cfb) --- using hourly PVGIS-SARAH3
data. Six design variables are jointly optimized via Differential Evolution
to maximize a novel three-component extended Land Equivalent Ratio,
$eLER = LER_{\text{crop}} + LER_{\text{PV}} + LER_{\text{biogas}}$.
Optimized eLER reaches 1.641--1.764, with $LER_{\text{2C}}$ alone
(1.030--1.088) confirming APV outperforms separate monocultures even
without biogas. A counter-intuitive result emerges: Freiburg, with the
lowest solar resource, achieves the highest eLER, driven by PAR
saturation penalizing high-irradiance sites. The optimizer converges to
the physical row-spacing boundary at all sites. A 4E analysis shows PV
yields of 1{,}002--1{,}433~MWh\,ha$^{-1}$, NPV of
\SIrange{134}{802}{k\$}, and IRR of \SIrange{11.0}{28.1}{\percent}
(S0), rising to \SIrange{12.7}{29.9}{\percent} under a carbon-credit
scenario. Growing-season water savings of \SIrange{81}{159}{mm}
represent \SIrange{15.6}{22.4}{\percent} of seasonal ET$_0$. All
results are model-based, cross-validated against independent
literature datasets and an external solar-modelling library; the
integrated APV--AD system has not been field-demonstrated.

\bigskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} agrivoltaics; anaerobic digestion; extended
land equivalent ratio; food--energy--water nexus; differential evolution;
semi-arid climate; PAR saturation; 4E analysis
\end{abstract}
